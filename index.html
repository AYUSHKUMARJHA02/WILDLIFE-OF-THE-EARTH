◆ PILLAR 01 ◆

Land Wildlife — Prithvi Jeevan

Only 8% of the Earth's land is true wildlife

The land wildlife is the wildlife of the 30% of the Earth. But this is also not the whole truth — because 22% of the land is occupied by humans, which is not under wildlife. So we have just 8% of the land for the wildlife of the land — the wild animals, the wild forest, the wild mountain, the wild tree. There are four kingdoms of land wildlife: snow, desert, grassland, and forest.

◆ Why the Word "Wild" ◆ When we discuss the wild, it does not mean we are abusing. These are the wrong words. Because wild is the true nature of the Earth. We have to understand and experience that — because this will give us wisdom and great knowledge for our life, even if we live beyond the wildlife.
◆ LAND 01 · SNOW ◆

Snow Wildlife

The ancient ice age — the first face of land wildlife

At the ancient time — much, much ancient — there was a period when the snow covered more than 90% of the land of the Earth: the Ice Age. This was truly amazing, and we have a lot of scientific evidence. The animals then were much more different from these times. But today's snow wildlife still carries that ancient spirit — Antarctica, the Arctic, the Himalayas. Life survives where even humans cannot.

Snow 01 · The King

Polar Bear The White Giant · Arctic

The polar bear is the great white wanderer of the Arctic. It walks on drifting ice, swims for miles, and waits for seals at breathing holes. Its fur is not white — it is transparent and hollow, made to hold warmth and reflect light. It is the true sovereign of the snow.

  • Lives on sea ice — hunting seals for survival.
  • Swims for days across open ocean.
  • Two layers of fur plus thick blubber keep it warm in −40°C.
  • Threatened by melting ice — the changing Earth.
Snow 02 · The Ghost

Snow Leopard The Ghost of the Mountains · Himalaya

The snow leopard is called the "ghost of the mountains" — a solitary hunter of the high Himalayas, so silent that even shepherds rarely see it. Its long tail balances it on cliffs; its fur holds warmth; its paws are snowshoes. It is the very spirit of the Indian Himalayan wilderness.

  • Hunts ibex, blue sheep, and marmots above 3,000 m.
  • Leaps up to 15 metres in a single pounce.
  • A long tail up to 1 metre wraps around it like a blanket.
  • Critically endangered — only a few thousand remain.
Snow 03 · The Stripe

Siberian Tiger Amur Tiger · Siberia

The Siberian tiger is the largest cat on Earth — a hunter of the frozen forests of Russia and China. Its fur is thicker and paler than its Bengal cousin; its body is built for snow. It walks alone through birch forests where wolves fear to pass.

  • Largest cat — up to 300 kg and 3.5 metres long.
  • Thick fur and a layer of fat survive −30°C winters.
  • Hunts boar, deer, and even small bears.
  • Fewer than 600 remain in the wild — an urgent sacredness.

◆ ANTARCTICA · THE ICE KINGDOM ◆

"Life endures where even the flame cannot."

Antarctica is the coldest, driest, windiest place on Earth. Yet life is there — Emperor penguins that stand through blizzards to protect one egg, Weddell seals that sing underwater, krill so many that they feed the entire ocean. This is the ancient snow wildlife that has never left us. It is the first face of the Earth's raw nature.

◆ LAND 02 · DESERT ◆

Desert Wildlife

Life that survives without water — the courage of the sand

The desert is where water vanishes but life does not. From the Sahara to the Kalahari, from the Thar of India to the deserts of Arabia, life has learned the deepest wisdom of survival — to live without water, to walk without shade, to endure without complaint.

Desert 01 · The Ship

Camel The Ship of the Desert · ऊँट

The camel is the greatest walking miracle of the desert. Its hump stores fat — not water — which can be converted to energy and moisture. Its eyelashes shield against sand; its nostrils close on demand; its feet are wide pads for sand. It can drink 100 litres in 10 minutes and then walk for days without a sip.

  • Survives 40°C heat and −20°C cold in the same desert.
  • Three eyelids — the third one is translucent, for sandstorms.
  • Drinks 100+ litres in a single long drink.
  • Walks 40 km/day in the Thar and the Sahara alike.
Desert 02 · The Giant

African Elephant The Desert Wanderer · Kalahari

The African elephant also lives in the Kalahari Desert and the Namib — the driest places of Africa. These desert elephants walk for kilometres through sand to find the next riverbed, digging for water with their trunks and remembering paths their grandmothers walked decades earlier.

  • Largest land mammal on Earth — up to 6,000 kg.
  • Remembers water sources across a lifetime.
  • Digs for water with trunk and tusks — creates wells for all wildlife.
  • Desert elephants walk hundreds of km a year.
Desert 03 · The Pride

Asiatic Lion Sinh · Gir, Gujarat, India

The Asiatic lion lives only in the Gir Forest of Gujarat, India — a semi-arid, dry deciduous region. It is smaller than the African lion, has a shorter mane, and lives in the land that is India's own desert border. It is the lion of the Sher, of our ancestors, of our own wildlife.

  • Only wild population — the Gir Forest of Gujarat.
  • Smaller mane, longer tail tuft than the African lion.
  • Lives in dry, scrub, and semi-arid jungle.
  • Around 700 only — protected with devotion.
◆ In India — The Desert Wildlife ◆ In India, you will see the environment of the Thar Desert of Rajasthan, and the Asiatic Lion living in the dry regions of Gujarat. The Great Indian Bustard — one of the heaviest flying birds — also lives in the desert and is now critically endangered. The desert is not empty — it is full of life that is silently heroic.
◆ LAND 03 · GRASSLAND ◆

Grassland Wildlife

The sea of grass — home of the greatest herds on Earth

When we discuss the grassland, there are many countries that are grasslands — the USA, Africa, India, Alaska, South America. There are many animals that live in the grasslands — the African lion, hyena, leopard. In the American grassland you will see the Grizzly Bear and the Kodiak Bear. In the grassland you will see the African buffalo, giraffe, and the African bush elephant — the world's largest land mammal.

Grassland 01 · The King

African Lion The King of the Grassland · सिंह

The African lion is the true sovereign of the grassland. It hunts in prides — the only big cat that lives in family groups. The male guards territory with a roar that carries 8 kilometres; the lionesses do most of the hunting, working as a team of silent shadows.

  • Lives in prides of up to 30 lions.
  • Roar heard up to 8 km away.
  • Lionesses hunt together — teamwork of survival.
  • The last true king of the African savanna.
Grassland 02 · The Tower

Giraffe The Tall Wanderer · जिराफ़

The giraffe is the tallest land animal on Earth — up to 5.5 metres. Its long neck reaches acacia leaves no other animal can eat. Its tongue is 45 cm long and bluish-purple to protect it from the sun. Its heart weighs 11 kg — the most powerful heart of any land animal, to pump blood up that tower.

  • Tallest land animal — up to 5.5 m.
  • Sleeps standing up, only 20 minutes to 2 hours a day.
  • Heart weighs 11 kg — pumps blood 2 metres upward.
  • Spotted pattern is unique — like a fingerprint.
Grassland 03 · The Giant

African Bush Elephant The Largest Land Mammal · हाथी

The African bush elephant is the world's largest land mammal — up to 6,000 kg and 4 metres tall. It is a gardener of the savanna: it knocks down trees to open grassland, spreads seeds in its dung, and digs water holes that all other animals drink from. When elephants go, the whole savanna suffers.

  • Largest land mammal — up to 6,000 kg.
  • Trunk has 40,000 muscles — can lift 300 kg.
  • Eats 150 kg of plants and drinks 200 litres a day.
  • Remembers water sources across decades.
Grassland 04 · American

Grizzly Bear & Kodiak Bear American Giants · Alaska

In the American grassland and the wild of Alaska, you will see the Grizzly Bear — and its giant cousin the Kodiak Bear, the largest brown bear on Earth. They fish in rivers for salmon, walk the tundra, and sleep through winter in dens they dug themselves. They are the shadow of North America.

  • Kodiak — largest brown bear — up to 700 kg.
  • Grizzly — claws 10 cm long, can run 55 km/h.
  • Sleeps through winter — the deep rest of the wild.
  • The great western bear of American wilderness.
◆ The Other Grassland Kings ◆ In the grassland of Africa, you will also see the African buffalo — one of the "Big Five," known for its unpredictable strength — and the hyena, the greatest scavenger-hunter of the savanna, with jaws strong enough to crack bone. And the leopard, who drags its prey up a tree to keep it from the lions. Every one of them is a lesson in survival.
◆ LAND 04 · FOREST ◆

Tropical Rainforest

The spirit of the jungle — Amazon and India

Now we discuss the rainforest, tropical rainforest. In the America you will see the Amazon — and there you will see the Jaguar, which is the spirit of the jungle. And in the Indian jungles you will see the Royal Bengal Tiger, which is the spirit of the Indian jungle.

Forest 01 · Spirit of Amazon

Jaguar The Spirit of the Jungle · Amazon

The jaguar is the spirit of the Amazon jungle. It is the only big cat that kills with a bite to the skull — piercing the brain directly. Its name means "he who kills in one leap." It swims, it climbs, it hunts caiman in rivers. Its rosettes — those dark circles with dots inside — are the signature of the Amazon's soul.

  • Third-largest cat — after lion and tiger.
  • Only big cat that kills by piercing the skull.
  • Swims rivers and hunts caiman — the crocodile's cousin.
  • Roars across the flooded forest of the Amazon.
Forest 02 · Spirit of India

Royal Bengal Tiger Spirit of the Indian Jungle · बाघ

The Royal Bengal Tiger is the spirit of the Indian jungle. It is India's national animal, the sovereign of the Sundarbans, the Ranthambore, the Kanha, the Jim Corbett. It hunts alone in silence; it swims rivers; it holds territories of 100 square kilometres. Its roar is the deepest song of the Indian wilderness.

  • National animal of India — the Sher of our soil.
  • Hunts alone — one silent hunter per 100 sq km.
  • Swims in rivers and even in the Sundarbans mangroves.
  • India still holds 75% of the world's wild tigers.

◆ THE FOREST · LUNGS OF THE EARTH ◆

"The jungle is not a place — it is the breath of the Earth."

The forest is the mother of life. The Amazon produces 20% of the oxygen we breathe — it is the lungs of the Earth. The Indian jungles are the home of the Royal Bengal Tiger, the Asiatic Elephant, the Indian Leopard, the Indian Rhino, the Sloth Bear, the Wild Dog. When the forest dies, the Earth's breath becomes shallow. So the forest must be protected as the lungs of our own body.

◆ PILLAR 02 ◆

Aquatic Wildlife — Jal Jeevan

The 50% contribution of the mountains and the ocean

Fifty percent of the contribution of the mountains and the ocean — because of the ocean there is rain, and because of the mountains there is glacier. Because of this, they give shape to the freshwater rivers. There are many lakes on the Earth — and they too are part of the freshwater. There is also salt water lakes, but we are discussing freshwater. Because of the freshwater — because of the river — there is aquatic life. And not only aquatic life — but also the migratory birds who live there, and the human lifeline — because the river water is the source of agriculture and human life itself.

◆ The Sacred Ganga and Yamuna ◆ In India, the Ganga and the Yamuna are not just rivers — they are mothers. They flow from the Himalayas, they carry the grace of the mountains, and they feed the plains of Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar — the most fertile lands of the world. The river is the source of the wheat, the rice, the sugarcane. Without the river there is no farm. Without the farm there is no food. Without the food there is no human. This is the simple truth — there is no rocket science.
Water 01 · The Fisher

Gharial & Crocodiles The Ancient River Kings · मगरमच्छ

The gharial is the most ancient of India's river creatures — a fish-eating crocodile with a long, narrow snout, uniquely designed for the flowing water of the Ganga and Chambal. Crocodiles in general are the oldest survivors of the river — unchanged for 200 million years. They are the guardians of the freshwater world.

  • Gharial — critically endangered — only ~650 remain.
  • Crocodiles — unchanged for 200 million years.
  • Mothers guard nests and carry hatchlings in their jaws.
  • Kept the rivers clean for aeons before humans.
Water 02 · The River Dolphin

Ganges River Dolphin The Blind Swimmer · गंगा डॉल्फिन

The Ganges river dolphin is nearly blind — it sees the world through sound. It swims the Ganga, Brahmaputra, and Meghna rivers, singing and listening, guiding itself through the dark water by echoes. It is India's national aquatic animal. When the river dies, the dolphin dies first — because it lives only in the living water.

  • India's national aquatic animal.
  • Nearly blind — sees with ultrasonic sound.
  • Lives only in freshwater — never in the sea.
  • Only ~4,000 remain — the river's sacred daughter.
Water 03 · The Hippo

Hippopotamus The River Horse · Africa

The hippopotamus lives in the rivers and lakes of Africa. Its name means "river horse" in Greek, but it is more closely related to whales. It can hold its breath for 5 minutes underwater. It walks the river bottom like a ballet dancer of 1,500 kg. It is the great river king of Africa — and without freshwater, it cannot survive.

  • Holds breath up to 5 minutes underwater.
  • Walks and runs along the river bottom.
  • Secretes a red "sunscreen" to protect its skin.
  • Kills more humans than any other African mammal — protect its water.
Water 04 · The Leaper

Fish, Mahseer & the Silent River The Life of Freshwater

The rivers carry thousands of species of fish. The Golden Mahseer of the Himalayan rivers is one of the world's largest freshwater fish — up to 2.5 metres. The rivers are also home to otters, turtles, freshwater crabs, and countless small creatures. Every drop of the river is alive.

  • Golden Mahseer — up to 2.5 metres — the sacred river fish.
  • Otters — playful hunters of the Himalayan rivers.
  • Turtles — ancient travellers of both water and land.
  • Every river species depends on the river's health.
◆ Our Responsibility to the Rivers ◆ The freshwater is the source of agriculture — because of the freshwater, the lands are fertile for the farm, for the field. If a region has many rivers, its land must be fertile — as Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar in India are the most fertile because of many rivers. That is why the human lifeline is also the river. And what are we doing? We are spoiling our rivers — our determination must be to maintain the beauty of our rivers, not to spoil, destroy, or disturb them.

If we cannot give the beauty to our river, then we do not have any right — we do not have any right to spoil our river — because we are snatching the life of the aquatic creature. The river is not ours to spoil. The river belongs to the fish, the dolphin, the crocodile, the gharial, the turtle — and only then to us.

◆ GANGA · THE MOTHER OF RIVERS ◆

"Ganga is not a river — Ganga is a living mother."

The Ganga flows 2,525 kilometres from the Gangotri Glacier in the Himalayas to the Bay of Bengal. She carries the grace of the mountains, feeds the plains, and holds a spiritual place in the heart of India that no other river in the world holds. The Ganga, the Yamuna, the Brahmaputra, the Godavari, the Narmada, the Krishna, the Kaveri — these are not rivers. These are the mothers of the Earth.

◆ PILLAR 03 ◆

Air Wildlife — Vayu Jeevan

The invisible element — the most unique beauty of all

Now the time for the air wildlife. Because of the atmosphere, because of the most important — the air. Because of this, if this will not happen, then we cannot survive even for seconds. That is the most important because air contains nitrogen, oxygen, and many gases which are essential for life. But the wisdom of the air is most unique — because the air is not visible, but we can feel it. That is her beauty. We can experience it. Because of this, we are surviving. Because of this, life on the Earth survives. That is the beauty of the air.

Air 01 · The Hunter of the Sky

Eagle The King of the Sky · चील / गरुड़

The eagle is the king of the sky. Its eyesight is 4–8 times sharper than a human's — it can see a rabbit from 3 kilometres away. It dives at 320 km/h. Its wings carry it to 3,000 metres above the Earth. In India, the eagle is Garuda — the vehicle of Lord Vishnu, the symbol of divine vision.

  • Vision 4–8× sharper than human — sees prey from 3 km.
  • Dives at 320 km/h — fastest of all birds.
  • Lives 20–30 years — some up to 50.
  • Garuda of Hindu tradition — the sacred sky-hunter.
Air 02 · The Night Hunter

Owl The Silent Hunter · उल्लू

The owl is the silent hunter of the night. Its wings have fringed edges that break the sound of air — so it flies in total silence. Its head rotates 270°. Its hearing is so precise that it can catch a mouse under 30 cm of snow. In India, the owl is the vehicle of Maa Lakshmi — the witness of wisdom.

  • Flies in total silence — fringed feathers kill the sound.
  • Head rotates 270° — no eyeballs, only eye tubes.
  • Hunts mice and rats — the farmer's friend.
  • Associated with Maa Lakshmi — the owl of wisdom.
Air 03 · The Purifier

Vulture — The Most Important Scavenger · गिद्ध

The vulture is the most important bird for our environment that many people do not realise. Actually, the vulture kills the vanished — kills the diseases. It eats the dead animals that would otherwise spread rabies, anthrax, and other diseases. In ancient India, Parsis placed their dead for vultures to purify. The vulture is not a dirty bird — the vulture is a sacred purifier. Without vultures, disease spreads.

  • Eats dead animals — stops rabies and anthrax spread.
  • Stomach acid pH 1 — kills almost all bacteria.
  • Soars on thermals for hours without flapping.
  • Vultures in India fell 99% due to a drug — a tragedy.
Air 04 · The Song

All Birds — Carnivore & Herbivore The Whole Family of the Air

There are lots of creatures in the air — from the carnivore to the herbivore. Carnivores like the big eagles, the owls, the falcons, the kites. Herbivores like the parrots, sparrows, pigeons, hummingbirds. And the scavengers like the vultures, crows, kites. Each one is essential. The parrot scatters seeds; the hummingbird pollinates; the owl hunts rodents; the vulture purifies. The sky is not empty — the sky is a whole city of life.

  • Parrots, sparrows, pigeons — the seed-spreaders.
  • Hummingbirds — the smallest birds, the jewel flyers.
  • Falcons — the fastest animals on Earth — 380 km/h.
  • Migratory birds — the travellers who cross continents.
◆ What Is Happening in Our Cities ◆ But what is happening in the cities, in the towns — we are not even seeing the sparrows, the small birds. They are dying from our pollution, air pollution. Because of human ego, because of human destructive mind — the ugly mind — the birds are no longer there. This is a very sorrowful experience for us. The sparrow was once the most common bird of every Indian home — now it is vanishing.

The birds are also the part of the Earth like us. We humans do not have any right to vanish their life, to kill their life.
